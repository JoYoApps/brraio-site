Atrial fibrillation vs atrial flutter: how to tell them apart

By Mitch, registered paramedic and founder of Brraio · 14 October 2026 · 5 min read

Atrial fibrillation and atrial flutter are both fast atrial rhythms, and they often occur in the same patient. They look different on the ECG, and recognising which one you are looking at helps with the next questions.

Atrial fibrillation (AF)

  • Rhythm: irregularly irregular. No two R to R gaps are the same.
  • P waves: none that you can identify. The baseline looks fine and wavy.
  • QRS: usually narrow, unless there is a bundle branch block.
  • Rate: the ventricular rate varies widely, from slow to very fast.

The quickest recognition cue is the pattern of the rhythm itself. If you cannot find a repeating pattern, think AF.

Atrial flutter

  • Rhythm: often regular, because the AV node lets through a fixed fraction of the atrial beats. It can be irregular if the block varies.
  • Flutter waves: a sawtooth pattern with an atrial rate close to 300 a minute. Look in leads II, III and aVF.
  • Ventricular rate: with 2 to 1 conduction it is close to 150 a minute and does not change. This is a classic hidden cause of a "regular tachycardia at 150".

Why it matters

Both rhythms raise the risk of stroke, and both need decisions about rate control, rhythm control and anticoagulation. The right choices depend on the patient and on your local guidelines, so treat this page as a pattern-recognition aid, not a treatment guide.

A practical tip

If a regular narrow tachycardia sits very close to 150 a minute, do not stop at "SVT". Look for flutter waves, because the rate that never changes is the giveaway. Our heart rate guide covers how to check the rate quickly.
