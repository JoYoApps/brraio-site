How to calculate heart rate from an ECG: 3 quick methods

By Mitch, registered paramedic and founder of Brraio · 14 October 2026 · 4 min read

Heart rate is step one of reading any ECG, and you can get it in seconds without a calculator. Which method you use depends on whether the rhythm is regular.

First, know your grid

At the standard paper speed of 25 mm/s:

  • 1 small box = 0.04 seconds
  • 1 big box (5 small boxes) = 0.2 seconds
  • 5 big boxes = 1 second
  • 30 big boxes = 6 seconds

Method 1: the 300 method (regular rhythms)

Find an R wave that lands on a heavy line. Count the big boxes to the next R wave and divide 300 by that number. Or just remember the sequence for each following heavy line: 300, 150, 100, 75, 60, 50.

Example: the next R wave lands 4 big boxes later, so the rate is 300 ÷ 4 = 75 beats a minute. This is what the strip above shows.

Method 2: the 6-second method (irregular rhythms)

Many 12-lead ECGs and rhythm strips carry markers for 3 or 6 seconds. Count the QRS complexes in 6 seconds (30 big boxes) and multiply by 10. If you have no markers, count the boxes yourself. This is the method to use in atrial fibrillation, where the R to R gap changes from beat to beat.

Method 3: the 1500 method (most accurate)

Count the small boxes between two R waves and divide 1500 by that number. It is the most precise method for a regular rhythm, which is handy at faster rates where the 300 method gets rough.

Atrial and ventricular rates can differ

In some rhythms the atria and ventricles beat at different rates, such as complete heart block or atrial flutter with block. Count the P waves and the QRS complexes separately and report both.

What counts as normal?

For adults, 60 to 100 beats a minute is the usual range at rest. Below 60 is bradycardia and above 100 is tachycardia, but always judge the number against the patient: an athlete asleep and an unwell patient with the same rate are very different pictures.
